Independent companion activity · Scrollway: Bible Study Game

Four books, two collections

Write Genesis, Ruth, Matthew and Acts on four separate cards. Ask an adult-guided group to place them under Old Testament or New Testament. Answer key: Genesis and Ruth belong under Old Testament; Matthew and Acts under New Testament. Next, ask everyone to use a Bible's contents page to check the placements. A correct guess counts as a starting point, while the contents page supplies the verification. Finish by choosing one of the four books and locating its opening page. No one needs to read aloud or explain their religious background. This is an independent paper activity, not a claim about a particular Scrollway level.

Independent companion activity · Wayfold: Understand the Bible

Evidence or extra assumption

Use this invented sentence, not a Bible quotation: "Mara returned before sunset. Eli arrived later, carrying an empty basket." Mark each claim as supported or not established: Mara arrived before Eli; Eli was angry; Eli carried a basket; the basket was empty because he lost something. Answer key: the first and third are supported; anger and the reason for the empty basket are not established. Underline the exact words that justify each supported claim. Then rewrite one unsupported claim as a question. The finished card shows the difference between a stated observation and an added assumption. The same checking habit can be used when reading a real passage, while respecting its wider context.

Independent companion activity · Lampstep: Bible for Real Life

Rewrite the reply around one request

Use this fictional draft: "You never tell me anything, so the whole plan is ruined." First underline the absolute claim, "never," and the conclusion, "ruined." Replace them with one observable fact and one request: "I did not see the changed start time. Could you confirm whether we are meeting at six or seven?" Compare the versions. The second does not decide what the other person intended; it identifies the missing information. Write one alternative that stays equally specific. Keep the exercise on paper and do not automatically send it. This is a communication practice for ordinary, safe situations, not guidance for handling abuse or an assurance of agreement.

Independent companion activity · Ninefruit: Fruit of the Spirit

Keep the request, remove the insult

Use this fictional sentence: "Only an idiot would leave all these folders on the desk." Identify the actual practical request hiding inside it: the folders need a storage place. Rewrite it as, "Could these folders go in the labeled drawer so the desk stays clear?" Now make another version that is equally direct but uses different wording. Check that both preserve the request and remove the personal attack. The completed exercise is two usable sample sentences, kept unsent. It concerns ordinary communication, not a method for resolving abuse or a requirement to contact someone unsafe. A clear boundary can remain firm without the insult.

Independent companion activity · FORGED: Daily Bible for Men

Capture a talk in three useful lines

After a talk, use three headings: passage or subject, idea to revisit, and question. Write one brief note beneath each. If a statement is being recalled approximately, mark it as a paraphrase rather than placing it in quotation marks. Check the Scripture reference before keeping it. The finished record should help the reader resume their own investigation later, without claiming to reproduce everything the speaker said. Personal reflections and the speaker's identity need not be included in a shared example.
