Build a two- or four-meeting reading plan ↓

Make a paired Advent reading plan

Give one reader Matthew and another Luke. Read the passages in your own Bible, then compare what each author includes. Choose two longer meetings or four shorter ones. These are discussion pairings rather than a proposed timeline.

All four reading pairs and questions
  1. Who hears the news?

    Matthew 1:18–25 and Luke 1:26–38

    Who receives a message in each account? Find one question or response that belongs to each person.

  2. Why Bethlehem?

    Matthew 2:1–6 and Luke 2:1–7

    Which passage describes a journey to Bethlehem? Which one describes a question about Bethlehem, and who asks it?

  3. Two visits

    Matthew 2:7–12 and Luke 2:8–20

    Who visits in each passage? Choose one action the visitors take and point to the verse that describes it.

  4. Leaving and returning

    Matthew 2:13–23 and Luke 2:22–39

    Where does each account end in these verses? Identify one movement each author describes. Leave the timing between the two accounts open.

Two Gospels tell the birth. Neither mentions a stable, an innkeeper, a donkey, three kings or a date. Here is what they do say, in order.

  1. Luke

    Nazareth: Gabriel comes to Mary

    Luke 1:26-38, World English Bible

    “Behold, you will conceive in your womb and give birth to a son, and shall name him ‘Jesus.’” (1:31)

    In the text
  2. Luke

    The hill country: Mary with Elizabeth

    Luke 1:39-56, World English Bible

    “Mary arose in those days and went into the hill country with haste, into a city of Judah.” (1:39)

    In the text
  3. Matthew

    Joseph’s dream

    Matthew 1:18-25, World English Bible

    “You shall name him Jesus, for it is he who shall save his people from their sins.” (1:21). Matthew tells the announcement from Joseph’s side; Luke from Mary’s.

    In the text
  4. Luke

    The census: Nazareth to Bethlehem

    Luke 2:1-5, World English Bible

    “A decree went out from Caesar Augustus that all the world should be enrolled.” (2:1) Joseph goes up “to David’s city, which is called Bethlehem” (2:4), with Mary, “being pregnant” (2:5). No donkey is mentioned.

    In the text
  5. Luke

    The birth

    Luke 2:6-7, World English Bible

    “She wrapped him in bands of cloth and laid him in a feeding trough, because there was no room for them in the inn.” (2:7) A feeding trough, an inn with no room. No stable, no innkeeper, no animals in the verse.

    In the text
  6. Luke

    The shepherds and the heavenly army

    Luke 2:8-20, World English Bible

    “There were shepherds in the same country staying in the field, and keeping watch by night over their flock.” (2:8) Then “a multitude of the heavenly army praising God and saying, ‘Glory to God in the highest…’” (2:13-14). Saying, in the text; singing is tradition.

    In the text
  7. Luke

    Day eight: named Jesus

    Luke 2:21, World English Bible

    “When eight days were fulfilled for the circumcision of the child, his name was called Jesus, which was given by the angel before he was conceived in the womb.”

    In the text
  8. Luke

    Jerusalem: Simeon and Anna

    Luke 2:22-39, World English Bible

    “They brought him up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord” (2:22). Afterwards, in Luke, “they returned into Galilee, to their own city, Nazareth” (2:39).

    In the text
  9. Matthew

    The wise men, Herod, the star, the house

    Matthew 2:1-12, World English Bible

    “Wise men from the east came to Jerusalem” (2:1). The star “went before them until it came and stood over where the young child was” (2:9). “They came into the house and saw the young child with Mary” (2:11). No number of wise men; three gifts.

    Debated: when
  10. Matthew

    Egypt by night, then Nazareth

    Matthew 2:13-23, World English Bible

    “He arose and took the young child and his mother by night and departed into Egypt” (2:14). Herod kills the boys “from two years old and under, according to the exact time which he had learned from the wise men” (2:16). Later they “came and lived in a city called Nazareth” (2:23).

    In the text

Order within each Gospel is the Gospel’s own. How Luke’s temple visit and Matthew’s wise men and Egypt fit together is not stated in either book, which is why that stop is marked Debated, the same label Wayfold uses in its notes.

Scratch the Christmas card

Eight things everyone knows about the first Christmas. Scratch each one to see whether it is in the text, a tradition, or neither.

  • Not in the text

    Matthew 2:1 says “wise men from the east”. No number; no “kings”; and by 2:11 they come “into the house”. The three comes from the three gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh.

  • Not in the text

    Luke 2:7: “because there was no room for them in the inn.” An inn, no innkeeper. Every innkeeper in every nativity play is an addition.

  • Not in the text

    Luke says “feeding trough” three times (2:7, 2:12, 2:16) and never says stable, ox or donkey. A trough implies animals somewhere; the text stops there.

  • Traditions differ

    Luke 2:13: “a multitude of the heavenly army praising God and saying”. Saying, in the World English Bible and the Greek. Singing is how the church has heard it for centuries.

  • Not in the text

    Luke’s shepherds find a baby in a feeding trough (2:16). Matthew’s wise men find a “young child” in “the house” (2:11), and Herod targets boys two and under from the time the star appeared (2:16). How long after, the text does not say.

  • Not in the text

    Luke 2:4-5: Joseph “went up from Galilee… to enroll himself with Mary… being pregnant.” How they travelled is not recorded.

  • Not in the text

    Neither Gospel gives a date or a season. The shepherds were “in the field… by night” (Luke 2:8); that is all the weather report there is.

  • In the text

    Matthew 2:9: the star “went before them until it came and stood over where the young child was.” That one is in. Over a house, not a stable.

Who this is not for

  • Anyone who wants a paraphrase.Wayfold quotes the World English Bible word for word, never a retelling. If you want the story in modern prose, that is a different kind of book.
  • Anyone expecting all four Gospels.Only Matthew and Luke tell the birth. Mark begins with John the Baptist; John begins before creation. The app will not invent a Mark 1 nativity for you.
  • Anyone who wants the app to settle the debates.Where the two accounts’ timing is uncertain, Wayfold says Debated and shows the sources. It does not pick a side for you.
  • Small children on their own.It is a study app for adults and groups. Parents can read the chapter aloud from it, with the built-in voice if they like.
