USE IT IN REAL LIFE

Carry the habit into your next Bible reading.

The scenes above teach ordinary reading checks. They do not settle the interpretation of a biblical passage. Before applying a verse, slow down for these questions:

  1. Read the surrounding passage. Look for the start and end of the conversation, argument or story. Avoid treating an isolated sentence as the whole message.
  2. Name the speaker and first audience. Is this narration, dialogue, a quotation within dialogue, or a letter addressed to a particular community?
  3. Keep the connecting words. Notice “if,” “because,” “therefore” and “but.” Ask what a pronoun refers to instead of filling the gap from memory.
  4. Notice the form of writing. Poetry, narrative, letters and proverbs can communicate differently. Do not assume every vivid image is a literal instruction.
  5. Separate what you see from what you infer. Write “the passage says…” separately from “I think this means…” and “one possible step is…”.

When a passage remains unclear

Write the question down. Consult the surrounding chapter, a trusted study resource, or a knowledgeable leader in your faith community. It is better to keep an honest question than manufacture certainty from a fragment.

A two-minute note

Text and context: passage reference, speaker, audience, one surrounding detail.
Open question: what you still need to understand.
Possible response: one specific, proportionate action, without claiming the passage promises an outcome it does not state.
