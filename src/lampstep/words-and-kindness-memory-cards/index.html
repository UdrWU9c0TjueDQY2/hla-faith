Keep practice small

  1. Read one verse and its surrounding passage.
  2. Try the two cards for that verse. Use a hint or reveal whenever useful.
  3. Say the whole verse with its reference once, then stop or continue at your own pace.
  4. Come back another day without treating a forgotten word as a failure.

What the recap means

“Entered correctly” means you supplied the matching word without revealing it on that card. It does not prove memorization: you may have used a hint or read the source. “Completed after reveal” means the answer was shown before that card was completed. Neither is a measure of faith, understanding or spiritual progress.

Keep the wording and the meaning together

A missing-word exercise is a starting point. Read the paragraph around each verse and consider a concrete response in its context. Do not use an isolated phrase to settle a complicated situation.

SCRIPTURE INTO PRACTICE

Rewrite one line for the person who will hear it

Read Ephesians 4:25–32, especially verse 29. Pay attention to what the listener needs. Then read Proverbs 12:18 and consider its contrast between reckless and wise speech. The three scenes below are original practice situations.

Pick a scene, write one sentence, then compare it with the worked example. Several replies could fit. Writing stays in this tab until you download or leave.

Correct a detail

A volunteer has printed a notice that says the doors open at six. They open at seven.

First draft: “You never check anything.”

Compare a worked revision

“The doors open at seven. Could we update the notice before we put it up?”

The revised sentence corrects the time without making a claim about the editor’s habits.

Give useful credit

After a busy supper, someone quietly packs the leftovers and labels the tubs.

First draft: “You’re great at everything.”

Compare a worked revision

“You marked each tub with its contents. That made it easy to send the right food home. Thank you.”

The thanks points to an observable action. It does not require the person to live up to a sweeping compliment.

Make a request

Two people are setting up a room. One has piled boxes in front of the stack of chairs you need.

First draft: “You’re always in my way.”

Compare a worked revision

“I need to reach the folding chairs. Could we move the two boxes beside the door?”

The listener can act on the request without having to defend their character.
