Book, chapter and verse: a simple reading guide

A reference points to a location in a biblical book. In John 3:16, John names the book, 3 names the chapter and 16 names the verse. It does not point to page 316; page numbers vary between editions.

Why does 1 John start with a number?

The numeral is part of the book name. John and 1 John are different books. Keep the whole name together before reading the chapter number.

What does the dash mean?

Ruth 1:16-17 requests verses 16 through 17, including both ends. These exercises use ranges inside one chapter. Other references may cross chapters or separate passages with punctuation; those formats are outside this short practice.

Take the next step in your own Bible

  1. Use the contents list to find the book.
  2. Find the chapter, then the verse number.
  3. Read the requested range, including its endpoints.
  4. Read nearby lines so the reference stays in context.

These are notation exercises, not scripture quotations or interpretations. Verse numbering can differ between editions. Consult the edition used by your group. Reference conventions: Concordia University Library SBL citation guidance.
