What "study" means in ours

How a chapter works, in three steps

  1. Read the chapter in the World English Bible, or have it read aloud.
  2. Answer a few questions about what happened, then see the exact words in the chapter that answer each one. Trace puts the main events in order, each tied to its verse.
  3. Go deeper, labelled. Short notes marked as what the text says, history, interpretation, or debated. Write a notebook entry; export it. Leading a group? Print the guide.

Who should not pick Wayfold

  • Anyone who needs a specific modern translation. It is the World English Bible only.
  • Anyone who wants the whole Bible as a study course. Six books, 21 chapters, is the catalogue today.
  • Greek and Hebrew word-study people. Blue Letter Bible does that for free and does it better.
  • Android. iPhone only.

Questions people ask

Which Bible study apps have no subscription at all?

Of the six here, YouVersion, Blue Letter Bible and BibleProject list no subscription. Wayfold has none either; its extra books are a one-time purchase. Logos and Olive Tree are free to use with optional subscriptions on top. All of this is from the App Store listings on 4 November 2026, and listings change; the links above go to the current text.

Is a free Bible app good enough for leading a small group?

For reading together, yes. For leading, you usually want questions, an answer key, and something to hand out. Olive Tree and Logos have study resources, some paid. Wayfold is built around that specific job, with printable leader guides per chapter or per book, and the Ruth and Jonah guides are free.

Why is your own app in the list?

Because leaving it out would be stranger than including it. It is labelled, it is last, and the quiz sends plenty of people to the others. We make Wayfold and we think it is the right choice for a narrow job; for most other jobs, one of the five above is better.

Which translation does Wayfold use?

The World English Bible, which is public domain, read on screen or aloud. If you need a specific modern translation for your group, YouVersion, Olive Tree and Logos carry far more versions.

Does Wayfold work offline?

The reader and study content do; the map tiles need a connection, per the listing. YouVersion and Olive Tree let you download versions for offline reading.

Did you test these apps yourselves?

We checked each listing on the date above and have used the free tiers. We have not paid for the subscriptions on Logos or Olive Tree and do not describe what is inside them beyond what their listings say. No ratings or review counts are quoted here on purpose; go and read them yourself.

The six, in a chat

Each "listing" line is from the App Store on 4 November 2026. We quote no ratings or download counts for anyone, including ourselves.

1. YouVersion Bible

What does the listing actually say about paying?

Free, with no subscription offered in the listing. Thousands of reading plans, 3,500+ versions, audio, and offline download for selected versions. Listing, checked 4 November 2026.

And honestly?

Reading, plans and audio, with friends. It is a Bible first and a study tool second: you will not find chapter questions with verse-level answers or an inductive workflow.

Pick it if you are: The person who wants to read daily and share a plan with a group.

2. Blue Letter Bible

What does the listing actually say about paying?

Free. The listing lists no subscription. Over 30 versions, text and audio commentaries, Hebrew and Greek lexicons, concordances and dictionaries. Listing, checked 4 November 2026.

And honestly?

Original-language depth for free is the headline. The trade is interface: it is dense, built for people who already know what a lexicon is.

Pick it if you are: Anyone who wants Strong's numbers and word studies without paying.

3. Logos Bible

What does the listing actually say about paying?

Free to download with over 40 free Bibles and books; optional Logos Plus and Premium subscriptions add AI features and more books. A free account is required. Listing, checked 4 November 2026.

And honestly?

The free tier is real and generous, and the subscription is optional. If you never want to be asked, this one will ask.

Pick it if you are: Sermon prep and serious library building, if you are fine with an account.

4. Olive Tree Bible Study

What does the listing actually say about paying?

Free app with five free major translations; optional Bible Study Pack subscriptions add tools and training. Everything downloads for offline use. Listing, checked 4 November 2026.

And honestly?

A strong free reader with a paid layer. The listing is clear that the packs are subscriptions, so read it if "never renews" matters to you.

Pick it if you are: Readers who want ESV or NIV offline and may buy study resources later.

5. BibleProject

What does the listing actually say about paying?

All free; the listing says there is no paid subscription. Videos, podcasts, classes and reading plans. Listing, checked 4 November 2026.

And honestly?

It teaches how the Bible fits together, beautifully. It is not a chapter-by-chapter study tool, and there is no leader guide or answer key.

Pick it if you are: Anyone who wants the big picture before, or alongside, close reading.

6. Wayfold (ours)

What does the listing actually say about paying?

Ruth and Jonah free, including their printable leader guides; four more books with a one-time Premium Lifetime. The listing says no subscription and nothing renews. Offline reader, 35 MB. Listing, checked 4 November 2026.

And honestly?

Narrow on purpose: six books, 21 chapters, each with questions whose answers are the exact verse. A map for every chapter that names a place, with how sure scholars are of each location. It is not a full-library app and does not pretend to be.

Pick it if you are: Someone leading a small group who wants questions, an answer key and a map, and who would rather pay once than monthly.

A Tuesday night group, week one

A composite scenario, not a customer story.

Maria has agreed to lead the Tuesday group through Jonah because nobody else would. She has four Bible apps on her phone and no idea which to open. One has a plan for Jonah that is mostly devotional. One has a lexicon she is slightly afraid of. One wants an account before it shows her anything.

What she needs by Tuesday is a printable with questions and answers, so that when Dave asks "where does it say that?" she can point at a verse rather than a feeling. She prints the Jonah 1 guide, reads the chapter on the bus, and on Tuesday night asks the trace question: put the storm, the lots, the confession and the overboard in order. Dave gets it wrong. The verse settles it. That is the whole evening, and it is a good one.

For her Thursday reading she still opens YouVersion. That is fine. That is what the list above is for.
