USE IT IN REAL LIFE

Keep the person informed, not guessing.

  1. Name the commitment. “Help with the event” is vague. “Make six name cards by Friday” can be checked.
  2. Notice a mismatch early. A change in capacity is useful information. Share it before someone relies on a plan you cannot keep.
  3. Offer a realistic alternative. A smaller task, different time or honest no can be clearer than a bigger promise made from guilt.
  4. Wait for agreement. Sending a new plan does not mean the other person accepted it.

In Christian reflection, faithfulness is one of the fruit named in Galatians 5:22–23. These examples are practical interpretations, not scripture quotations. A commitment never requires remaining in an unsafe situation.
