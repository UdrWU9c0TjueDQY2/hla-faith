01

Meetings

Love

Make room for an unfinished thought

A colleague starts an idea, but the conversation moves on before they finish. You are not leading the meeting, and you also have something to add.

How could you make room for their contribution without speaking on their behalf?

Explore a possible response
Before my point, I would like to hear the rest of that idea, if you want to finish it.

Look back: Did I leave the person free to speak or pass? Did I listen when they answered?

02

Shared tasks

Joy

Let someone else have the good news

Someone on the team reaches a milestone. Your own project has stalled, and the announcement brings up mixed feelings.

What could you appreciate about their work without pretending your own disappointment has disappeared?

Explore a possible response
That took persistence. I am glad this part came together for you.

Look back: What specific effort can I recognize? Joy here is an invitation to notice good, not a demand to feel cheerful.

03

Meetings

Peace

Name the decision underneath the disagreement

Two people disagree about the order of a routine project. The discussion is becoming personal, but there is a concrete scheduling decision to make.

What shared question could bring the conversation back to the task?

Explore a possible response
Can we list what each deadline needs, then choose the order from that?

Look back: Did I help clarify the issue, or simply try to end an uncomfortable conversation? Peace does not require hiding a real concern.

04

Messages

Patience

Ask for an update without adding a sting

A routine reply has not arrived. You do not know whether the other person saw your message. Your next step depends on their answer.

What information is actually missing, and when do you need it?

Explore a possible response
Checking that this reached you. I need the file choice by Thursday to prepare the draft. Is that workable?

Look back: Was the deadline clear? Did I separate the missing reply from a guess about the other person's motives?

05

Shared tasks

Kindness

Make a handoff easier to receive

You are passing a shared task to someone who has not done it before. You remember the confusing part, but the existing notes skip over it.

What small piece of context would have helped you the first time?

Explore a possible response
The latest version is in this folder. Step three is easy to miss; I added a short example beside it.

Look back: Did the extra note reduce a real point of confusion? Ask what would help instead of assuming a whole new system is needed.

06

Shared tasks

Goodness

Correct the small detail before it spreads

You notice that a routine project summary credits you for a task completed by someone else. No one has asked you about it.

What accurate correction can you make while the summary is still easy to change?

Explore a possible response
One correction before this goes out: that section was prepared by my colleague, not me.

Look back: What needs to be made accurate? Keep the correction specific rather than turning it into a speech about your character.

07

Shared tasks

Faithfulness

Renegotiate a promise before it slips

You agreed to send a first draft on Friday. Another task took longer than expected, and the original promise is now unlikely.

What can you reliably offer, and how early can you make the change visible?

Explore a possible response
I cannot finish the full draft by Friday. I can send the outline then and the draft on Monday. Would that still help?

Look back: Did I offer a realistic next step? Following through includes being honest when the original commitment needs to change.

08

Messages

Gentleness

Keep the correction; soften the delivery

A shared document has a repeated formatting mistake. Your first draft of the message says, "You always do this wrong."

Can you name the fix without labeling the person?

Explore a possible response
These headings use two different styles. Could we use the same style in both sections?

Look back: Is the request still clear? Gentleness can be direct; it does not require making the correction vague.

09

Messages

Self-control

Let the first draft stay a draft

A short message sounds dismissive to you. You have typed a sharp reply, but you have not sent it. The exchange is not urgent.

What would be useful to understand before you choose your response?

Explore a possible response
I may be reading this differently than you intended. Are you asking for a shorter version, or a different direction?

Look back: What was observed, and what did I infer? A pause gives you another choice; it does not guarantee the conversation will go well.

Make the conversation useful

A short discussion, without putting anyone on the spot.

For a voluntary small group

  1. Read the fictional situation aloud. Anyone may pass, and no one needs to share an experience from work.
  2. Allow a quiet moment to consider the question. Ask for different possible responses before opening the example.
  3. Compare the responses with the passage and the circumstances. Ask what each response makes clearer, and what it might miss.
  4. End with one optional idea to try. Leave personal commitments with the person who makes them.

Use these with people who choose to participate. They are not an employee assessment or a way to judge a colleague's beliefs.

For a private reflection

One moment: What happened, without guessing the other person's motives?

One response: What is within my control, and what would fit the situation?

One later look: What did I try? What remains unresolved?

Use paper if you want to keep a note. This page has no journal fields and saves no answers. You can also leave with an open question.

These cards concern routine interactions. Kindness, peace and gentleness do not require ignoring mistreatment, unsafe conditions or a serious concern. Use appropriate support and reporting channels when a situation calls for them.
