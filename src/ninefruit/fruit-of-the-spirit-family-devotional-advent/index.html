What are the nine fruit of the Spirit?

Love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness and self-control, from Galatians 5:22-23. The World English Bible, which Ninefruit quotes word for word, reads: “But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, and self-control.”

How do you do a fruit of the Spirit devotional with kids?

One fruit, one real situation, one small thing to try, two minutes. Not a lesson on all nine. The calendar below runs that shape for 24 days; the app runs it every day of the year with a Beginner, Teen or Adult reading style and family conversation cards.

Three steps, every evening

  1. Scratch the day

    A child scratches the foil (below, or on paper under a sticker). One fruit, one practice. No reading ahead.

  2. Do the two minutes

    The practice is small on purpose: a note, a pause, a jar, a job. Do it at the table or right after. In the app, Today’s Moment gives a real situation with choices first.

  3. Say what you saw

    One sentence each: where did that fruit show up today, in someone else? Point to the person. That is the whole devotional.

Day 4, made up but familiar

6:40 pmThe seven-year-old scratches the foil with a spoon handle and reads “Patience” with disgust. The practice is The Pause: count three before answering when someone interrupts.

6:41 pmShe is interrupted within the minute by her brother. She counts three, loudly, on her fingers, then answers. The brother is outraged that it worked.

7:15 pmAt “say what you saw”, the brother says he saw patience when Dad did not shout about the spilled juice. Dad had not noticed he was being patient. That is the devotional working.

LaterIn the app, the adult does Today’s Moment alone: a coworker takes credit; three choices; the verse; one thing to try tomorrow. Two minutes. The orchard’s patience tree moves to Sprout.
